Production Throughput & Bottleneck Analysis · IE 4933 · Spring 2026 · Solo

Tracing a plant's bottleneck to machine-fault downtime

A Power BI analysis of six months of production data from a fictional plant, Magnolia Manufacturing. It found which workstation was limiting output and traced the cause to machine-fault downtime, not scrap.

The problem

The plant was missing its output targets. My goal was to find which part of the production system was limiting throughput and recommend what plant leadership should do about it.

Data & tools

Five raw CSV files covering September 2025 to February 2026: production, labor, downtime, shift, and workstation data for six workstations. I cleaned them and built a relational data model in Power BI, plus a Date table.

What I did

  • Wrote DAX measures for Unit Target %, Gross Attainment Rate, Scrap Rate, Units per Labor Hour, Downtime % by Workstation, and Total Downtime.
  • Built a one-page interactive dashboard with KPI cards, workstation performance vs. downtime, a monthly target trend, downtime by category, and a shift comparison.
  • Wrote a one-page executive summary with findings and recommendations for plant leadership.

Findings

  • Drilling was the bottleneck, reaching 83.87% of its unit target while every other workstation was near 100%. The gap held across day and night shifts for all six months, so it was systemic, not a one-time disruption.
  • Drilling also had the lowest productivity, at 13.19 units per labor hour.
  • Scrap was ruled out: 1.8% at Drilling vs. about 1.2% elsewhere is far too small to explain the gap.
  • The root cause was downtime. Drilling was down 22.98% of the time, compared with 15.61% at Inspection, the next highest. Machine faults were the leading cause, at about three times the machine-fault downtime of any other station, and material shortages were also more frequent.

Recommendations

  • Start a preventive maintenance program for the Drilling equipment.
  • Run a formal inspection to decide between repairing and replacing it.
  • Improve material availability at Drilling to cut shortage downtime.

What I learned

Test the obvious explanation before acting on it. Drilling had the highest scrap rate, which made quality the easy guess, but the numbers pointed to availability instead.
