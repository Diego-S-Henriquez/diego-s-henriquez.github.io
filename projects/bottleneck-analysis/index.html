Production Throughput & Bottleneck Analysis

A Power BI root-cause analysis of six months of production data.

Power BIDAXData modelingOEERoot cause analysis

At a glance

Problem

A fictional six-workstation plant was missing its output targets. I had six months of production, labor, downtime, and shift data.

What I did

Cleaned 5 datasets, built a Power BI model with DAX and OEE measures, and tested scrap, downtime, cycle time, and shift as possible causes.

Result

Drilling was the bottleneck (83.9% of target, 75.4% OEE), driven by machine-fault downtime at 2.6× the next station. I recommended preventive maintenance and a material replenishment system.

The problem

The plant was missing its output targets. My goal was to find which part of the production system was limiting throughput and recommend what plant leadership should do about it.

Data & tools

Five raw CSV exports covering September 2025 to February 2026: production, labor, downtime, date, and workstation data for six workstations. The raw files were messy, so I cleaned them in Excel before building a relational data model in Power BI.

About 5,160 raw rows became 5,124 clean ones. The main fixes: splitting combined columns (shift and crew in one field, station and downtime reason in another), standardizing mixed date formats, station IDs and reason spellings, turning text like “32 hours” or “1,035” into numbers, filling recoverable blanks from the data (a missing target from the station's fixed target, a missing crew from the shift), and removing duplicates and report header rows.

What I did

  • Built a one-page interactive dashboard with KPI cards, output vs. downtime by workstation, downtime by cause, a monthly target trend, a shift comparison, and an OEE breakdown table.
  • Wrote a one-page executive summary with findings and recommendations for plant leadership.
  • Revisited the model after the course: corrected the downtime measure to count scheduled time on both shifts, and added the OEE breakdown.

Key DAX measures

Three of the measures behind the dashboard, copied from the Power BI file: downtime as a share of scheduled time, OEE from its three parts, and a measure that names the lowest-OEE workstation automatically.

Show the DAX

Downtime % of scheduled time

Each production row is one workstation on one 8-hour shift, so scheduled minutes are the row count times 480. Downtime is measured against that.

Scheduled_Min = COUNTROWS ( ProductionData ) * 480

Downtime % = DIVIDE ( [Total_Downtime_Min], [Scheduled_Min] )

OEE from its three parts

Availability is run time over scheduled time. Performance compares the ideal run time at each station's standard cycle time with the time it actually ran. Quality is good units over total units. OEE multiplies the three, which shows why a station is behind, not just that it is.

Availability = DIVIDE ( [Scheduled_Min] - [Total_Downtime_Min], [Scheduled_Min] )

Performance =
DIVIDE (
    SUMX (
        ProductionData,
        ProductionData[Units_Produced]
            * RELATED ( WorkstationInfo[Standard_Cycle_Time_Seconds] )
    ) / 60,
    [Scheduled_Min] - [Total_Downtime_Min]
)

Quality = DIVIDE ( SUM ( ProductionData[Good_Units] ), [Total_Units_Produced] )

OEE = [Availability] * [Performance] * [Quality]

Finding the bottleneck automatically

Scores every workstation's OEE in the current filter context and returns the lowest, so the KPI card names the constraint on its own and updates when you filter by shift.

Lowest OEE Station =
VAR t = ADDCOLUMNS ( VALUES ( WorkstationInfo[Process_Name] ), "@oee", [OEE] )
VAR worst = TOPN ( 1, t, [@oee], ASC )
RETURN MAXX ( worst, WorkstationInfo[Process_Name] & ": " & FORMAT ( [@oee], "0.0%" ) )

Findings

  • Drilling was the bottleneck, reaching 83.9% of its unit target while the other workstations ranged from 95.2% to 104.0%. (Targets are set at 90% of each station's ideal output, which is why a station can beat 100%.) It was the lowest station in every month and on both shifts, so the gap was systemic, not a one-time disruption.
  • Scrap was ruled out: 1.9% at Drilling vs. 1.2–1.5% elsewhere is far too small to explain the gap.
  • The main cause was downtime. Drilling was down 14.9% of its scheduled time, compared with 9.3% at Inspection, the next highest. Machine faults were the largest cause, at 2.6 times the machine-fault downtime of the next-highest station, and material-shortage downtime was more than four times any other station's.
  • Slower cycles were a second factor. Drilling's cycles averaged 56.1 seconds against a 52-second standard, about 8% slow, while every other station ran within 2% of standard.
  • OEE put it in one number: 75.4% at Drilling, the lowest in the plant (85.7–93.5% elsewhere), with availability at 85.1%, performance at 90.3%, and quality at 98.1%.
  • What fixing it is worth: Drilling finished 29,076 good units short of target over the six months. Bringing its machine-fault downtime down to Cutting's level would recover about 7,000 minutes, roughly 7,500 units at Drilling's actual cycle time, or about a quarter of the shortfall. (My estimate from the same data.)

Recommendations

  • Preventive maintenance for Drilling. Machine faults were Drilling's largest source of downtime, 11,358 minutes, or 2.6 times the next-highest station. The problem is how often it breaks, not how long repairs take: 291 fault events vs. 130 at Cutting, each lasting about the same (39 vs. 33 minutes on average). Preventing failures targets exactly that.
  • A repair-or-replace inspection. A formal inspection of the Drilling equipment to decide whether repair is enough or replacement makes more sense.
  • A material replenishment system. Material shortages cost Drilling 7,805 minutes, more than four times any other station. Inventory tracking with predefined reorder levels would restock materials before they run out, reducing downtime from shortages.
  • A time study of the Drilling operation. Drilling's cycles averaged 56.1 seconds against a 52-second standard, about 8% slow, while every other station ran within 2% of standard. A time study would show which elements run long and whether the method or the standard needs to change.

What I learned

Test the obvious explanation before acting on it. Drilling had the highest scrap rate, which made quality the easy guess, but the numbers pointed to availability instead.
